What gave it away

Eight tells, and what replaced them

1Furniture that never moves.A lower third, a ticker and a logo sat on screen for the whole video. Now nothing stays on screen unless it's doing something. The only fixed item is the #EpicPartner tag, because Epic requires it.
2Silence.A voice from frame one, with captions timed to it word by word, the way CapCut auto-captions look.
3Headlines nobody says out loud.The script is what you'd say to a friend in a lobby. The captions are the voice, not a separate headline.
4Everything eases in the same way.Motion follows the voice: a quick zoom on "right here", a whip pan on "and Pinky", a camera flash on "screenshot this".
5Perfect vectors under flat light.Film grain, a little handheld drift, motion blur when the camera moves fast.
6Graphics appear fully formed.Circles, arrows and labels get drawn on, lopsided like a real marker.
7A sponsor card that shouts.The code is one spoken line at the end plus a two-second card. It reads like a creator asking, not an ad.
8Every video built the same way.Each series gets its own move: a count-up for lists, a reveal for secrets, steps for how-tos.

The best free voice is yours

A real voice beats every model, and it costs nothing: record the script on your phone and drop it in. The captions and edit retime to any recording. Use the model voice for volume, and your own voice for the posts you expect to be biggest.

Other free options

TikTok and CapCut built-in voices: free, natural, and familiar to viewers, but they only work inside those apps. Piper (open source) is free but sounds flatter than Kokoro. ElevenLabs' free tier isn't an option here: it's non-commercial and needs attribution.

Decide on AI labels before posting

TikTok and YouTube both ask creators to label realistic AI-generated content, and a synthetic narrator may count. Your current rule is no AI labels. So either narrate yourself (no label question), or use the model voice and follow each platform's labeling rule. That's your call; nothing posts until you decide.

How the voice is made

The model reads the script one natural phrase at a time. Each phrase is measured, trimmed and placed on the timeline, and words are timed by their sounds. That's what drives the word-by-word captions and the camera moves. One command turns a script into a voiced, captioned, checked video.

How a video gets made now

Six steps, one approval

1PullEpic's news, map, locations and build, saved with the time.
2ScriptShort phrases a person would say. Every claim linked to the saved data.
3VoiceKokoro reads it; word timings come out with it.
4EditCamera moves, circles and captions land on the words.
5CheckFacts, spelling, frames, audio (-14 LUFS), safe zones, disclosure.
6YouFirst 5 of each series come to you. Nothing posts without a yes.

Your voice

Record two or three scripts yourself and compare them with af_heart on the same video. That test will tell you more than any setting.

Epic's image CDN is blocked from this session, so the official news images aren't used yet. Allowing that host in the environment's network settings fixes it.
