## Part A: The cartoon system

### A1. Why caricatures instead of "exact" likenesses
You want viewers to instantly recognize Colonel Sanders or Walt Disney. The best way to do that safely is **recognizable caricature**: the right era, silhouette, hair, glasses, clothing and signature props, drawn in one consistent BrandPros style. We deliberately avoid photorealistic or "exact copy" images because:
1. **Platform rules.** YouTube, TikTok and Meta require labels on *realistic* AI depictions of real people and events. A clearly cartoon style generally doesn't trigger that, and it never risks being mistaken for real footage.
2. **Likeness and defamation risk.** A realistic image of a living executive in a scene they were never in can read as a factual claim. A cartoon reads as commentary.
3. **Brand consistency.** One house style across hundreds of videos is what makes the channel recognizable and clearly original.
4. **Tool reliability.** Many image models refuse or distort named real people. Describing traits, and reusing an approved character sheet, works reliably and consistently.

*(This is practical guidance, not legal advice. For anything used in ads, merch or sponsor segments, ask a lawyer.)*

### A2. Likeness rules

| Subject | Allowed | Not allowed |
|---|---|---|
| **Deceased historical figures** (Sanders, Disney, Woolworth, the Dassler brothers, Hershey, Ford, Kroc) | Expressive caricature, era-accurate, playful | Mocking real tragedies; inventing quotes in speech bubbles |
| **Living public figures** (executives and founders in the news) | Recognizable, **neutral-to-friendly** caricature in editorial context, only in scenes depicting documented events | Unflattering exaggeration, depicting them doing things they didn't do, **any use in thumbnails, ads, merch or sponsor reads that implies endorsement** |
| **Private individuals** (employees, customers in a story) | Generic anonymous characters | Recognizable depictions |
| **Brands and logos** | Generic cartoon storefronts and products using the brand's **color scheme** as a hint; the official logo **overlaid unaltered** in assembly (never generated by the AI) | AI-drawn or altered logos; recreating trademarked **mascots or characters** (Geoffrey, Ronald, Mickey, minifigures) |

### A3. House style prompt (prefix every image prompt with this)
```
BrandPros house style: bold 2D editorial cartoon illustration, thick dark navy (#0E1A2B) outlines,
flat colors with one soft cel shadow, slightly oversized heads and expressive faces, playful exaggerated props,
limited palette of navy #0E1A2B, gold #F4B942, red #E63946, green #2BB673, cream #F5EFE0, gray #8A94A6,
clean simple backgrounds, subtle paper grain texture, vertical 9:16 composition with the subject in the upper two-thirds
(leave the bottom 25% calm for captions). No text, no letters, no logos, no watermarks, not photorealistic.
```
For long-form, swap the last line to `horizontal 16:9 composition`. For Dead Brands "glory days" scenes, append `warm sepia-toned vintage print look`.

**Negative prompt / exclusions:** `photorealistic, 3D render, real photograph, text, letters, logo, brand name, watermark, signature, extra fingers, distorted face, gore`.

### A4. Character sheets (for consistent characters)
Every recurring person gets a **character sheet**, approved once by a human and then reused as the **reference image** for every scene. That's how the same Colonel Sanders looks identical across 20 videos.

**Character sheet prompt template:**
```
[HOUSE STYLE PREFIX]
Character turnaround sheet of [ROLE DESCRIPTION], [ERA], [AGE RANGE],
[HAIR], [FACIAL HAIR], [GLASSES/ACCESSORIES], [CLOTHING], [SIGNATURE PROP],
[PERSONALITY/EXPRESSION], shown front, three-quarter and side views plus 4 expressions
(happy, shocked, thinking, determined), plain cream background.
```

### A5. Scene prompt template
```
[HOUSE STYLE PREFIX]
Scene: [the "Visual / cartoon scene" text from the script beat].
Characters: [character name] matching the attached reference sheet, [expression].
Setting: [place], [year/era details].
Camera: [close-up | medium | wide], [angle].
Mood: [series mood: tense red for $1B Mistake / warm sepia for Dead Brands / bright gold for How They Make Money / hopeful green for The Comeback / vintage navy-gold for Founder Files / high-energy split red-navy for Brand Wars].
```

### A6. Motion: making stills feel animated
- Every still gets automatic **pan/zoom plus parallax** (the foreground character separated from the background).
- **The Pro** gets a small set of reusable animated poses (point, shrug, gasp, stamp), made once and reused. Those reusable brand assets are fine; the per-story scenes are always new.
- 1–2 "hero" moments per video can use **AI image-to-video** (3–5 s) for extra life: a storefront's lights switching off, a price tag cracking.
- Charts, maps, timelines and price tags are built as **animated templates** (code or Creatomate), not AI images, so the numbers are always exactly right.

## Part B: The automation pipeline

### B1. Architecture
```
 Airtable production board  (one row per video; the Status column drives everything)
        │  status = "Approved idea"
        ▼
 Orchestrator (n8n or Make, or a small Python worker on a schedule)
        ├─► Claude API: research pack → script draft (series template) ─► ✋ GATE 1 (human edits the script)
        ├─► Claude API: skeptical fact-check pass ─────────────────────► ✋ GATE 2 (human signs off facts)
        ├─► ElevenLabs API: voiceover plus word timestamps
        ├─► Image API: scene prompts (house style + character sheets) → 2 options each → vision QC
        ├─► Render engine (Remotion or Creatomate): scenes + VO + captions + charts + music
        │        → vertical master (Shorts) / horizontal master (long-form)
        ├─► Thumbnail API (Bannerbear/Placid): 2 variants
        ├─► Google Drive: every asset saved in /BrandPros/<ID>/ ───────► ✋ GATE 3 (final phone watch)
        └─► Metricool: scheduled posts ×4 networks with per-platform captions from 07-content-calendar.csv
                 ▲
 Weekly job: Metricool analytics → KPI sheet → suggested next ideas (11-kpis.md rules)
```

### B2. Tool options for the cartoon images

| Option | Consistency method | Automatable via API? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Google Gemini image models** | Multiple reference images per prompt | ✅ | Strong at keeping a character consistent across scenes [VERIFY current model and pricing] |
| **OpenAI image API** | Reference image input and editing | ✅ | Good prompt-following; reliable text-free scenes [VERIFY] |

**Recommendation:** in **week 0**, run a bake-off. Generate the same 10 scenes (from S01, S05, S10) with each API option, using the style prefix plus 3 character sheets. Pick the winner on consistency, zero garbled text, and cost. Then optionally train a FLUX style LoRA on your approved images to lock the look permanently.

### B5. What stays human (and why)

| Gate | Time | Why it can't be skipped |
|---|---|---|
| Gate 1: script POV edit | ~20 min Short | The thesis, hook and "Pros know" lines are the originality YouTube's policy looks for |
| Gate 2: fact sign-off | ~15 min Short | Legal safety with real companies and people; credibility |
| Gate 3: final watch | ~5 min Short | Catches garbled AI text, off-model characters and accidental logos |

Total: about **45–55 minutes of human time per Short**, down from roughly 4.5 hours manual (see `08-production-workflow.md`).
